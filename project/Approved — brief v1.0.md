# Tally Desk — brief
Version: v1.0 · Date: 2026-11-06 · Time: 15:32 · Created by: Sheida
Sources: the Persian brief and the app in "Tally Desk Demo App v1.html"; Tally Desk Intro Video v2

## 1. Company and app
Tally Desk is a small software company. Its desktop app is for freelancers (designers, translators, developers) and small companies of up to ten people who create invoices, send them to clients and follow up until the money arrives. The app was built for computers only, and users often use it while working on their own projects.

## 2. Why now
The support team hears several kinds of complaint: creating an invoice takes long; some users find a mistake after sending; following up on payment is hard. The company does not know if these are one problem or several, or which matters most, so it wants to understand the problem before any design.

## 3. Scope
- In: the flow from creating and sending an invoice to getting paid.
- Out: the rest of the app (reports, settings, clients as a list).

## 4. The flow today (according to the company)
1. Create a new invoice
2. Pick or add a client
3. Add items and prices
4. Tax and discount
5. Preview and send by email
6. Track payment status and remind

## 5. What we know (K) — source: company
- K1: The app works only on desktop.
- K2: Users are mostly freelancers or companies of up to ten people.
- K3: Creating an invoice has six steps (steps 1–6).
- K4: Some invoices are paid late.
- K5: Payment reminders are currently sent by hand.
- K6: Clients receive invoices by email.
- K7: Some users send an invoice and only later notice a mistake in it.
- K8: There is no reliable data on why invoices are paid late.
- K9: The company named another invoicing tool only as an example, not as a need.
- K10: The company does not yet know where the biggest problem is.

## 6. Known from the demo app (K) — source: Tally Desk Demo App v1
These describe how the made-up app works, not how users behave.
- K11: The invoice number is set by the app and can't be changed.
- K12: Payment terms are 7, 14 or 30 days; the due date is worked out from the issue date.
- K13: In step 2, adding a new client opens a long form (name, contact, email, address).
- K14: In step 4, tax is a percentage and discount is a fixed amount in €.
- K15: In step 5, the email's To, Subject and Message are filled in for you; the invoice is attached as a PDF.
- K16: A sent invoice can't be edited. The app says to void it and create a new one, but the demo shows no Void button.
- K17: The app does not send reminders on its own. The user writes each reminder's subject and message from empty fields.
- K18: After the due date the status becomes Overdue and the activity log shows "Payment is late. No reminder sent yet."
- K19: Payment is marked as paid by hand ("Mark as paid").
- K20: An invoice can be saved as a draft during creation.

## 7. Research questions (RQ)
- RQ1: In which of steps 1–5 do users get stuck or make mistakes? (from K3, K7)
- RQ2: Why are invoices paid late, from the sender's view and the receiver's view? (from K4, K8)
- RQ3: How much of the user's time do manual reminders take, and how do they do them? (from K5)
- RQ4: After sending, how do users notice a mistake and how do they fix it? (from K7)
- RQ5: How do existing invoicing tools, starting with the one the company named, handle the flow from creating to follow-up? (from K9)
- RQ6: What does published research say about late invoice payment and the effect of reminders? (from K4, K5)

## 8. Not evidence
- The Mina scenario is made-up: a freelance designer finishes a project at month end, creates and sends an invoice, notices a few hours later she left out an item, doesn't know how to fix a sent invoice, and days later must remind the client by hand. We use it only to think about the flow.
